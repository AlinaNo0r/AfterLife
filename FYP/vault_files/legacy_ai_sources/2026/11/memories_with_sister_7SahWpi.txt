When Noori was born I was eleven years old. I remember holding her for 
the first time and being scared because she was so tiny. She had a loud 
cry even as a baby. As a child she followed me everywhere, even to the 
cricket ground, where she sat on a brick and kept score in her notebook. 
She always wanted my last piece of chocolate and I always gave it to her, 
though I pretended to be angry. When she was seven she cried because I 
went to college in another city. I promised I would call every Sunday, 
and I kept that promise for twenty years.
When our father passed away, Noori was only sixteen. She did not eat for 
two days. I sat beside her on the roof, where we used to fly kites, and 
told her that I was not going anywhere. I decided that day that her 
education would never stop. I took extra work in the evenings to pay her 
college fees. She never knew how tight money was, and I never told her. 
She topped her class in her second year and ran to call me before even 
telling her friends. That was one of the proudest moments of my life.
At Noori's wedding I stood in for our father. When she walked in wearing 
red, I had to look away because I was crying. She held my hand and 
whispered, "Bhai jaan, don't cry, my makeup will run." Then we both 
laughed. I told her husband Imran to take care of her, and that if he 
made her cry, I would come to his house with my cricket bat. He still 
jokes about it. Before she left, I told her this house will always be 
your home, and my door is always open.
Every Sunday evening Noori called me, and the first thing she asked was 
"Bhai jaan, khana kha liya?" She worried that I skipped meals. I used to 
tease her about her cooking. Once she made biryani that was so salty, we 
both pretended it was fine and drank three glasses of water. After that, 
whenever she cooked something, I would say "I'll need extra water, 
beta." She would complain and then laugh. She always said I sang badly, 
and she was right.
Noori, you worry too much. Most of the things you are scared of never 
happen. When you are afraid, take a deep breath and call someone you 
trust. Be patient with Imran, and be patient with yourself. Never be 
ashamed to ask for help. Keep studying and keep your own identity. 
Your strength is much greater than you think. And please practice your 
cooking, at least for the sake of your children.

One thing I regret is missing Noori's graduation ceremony because of a 
work deadline. She said she understood, but I saw the disappointment in 
her face. I made it up to her with a dinner, but I still feel bad. 
I hope she knows that nothing in my life mattered more than her 
happiness.
I believe Allah never gives a burden bigger than what we can carry. 
After our father passed away, I prayed Fajr every day and asked for 
strength for Noori and me. I believe good deeds come back to you, 
sometimes years later. I believe family is the only real wealth. 
Noori, when life feels heavy, remember that every hard night ends in 
a morning. Dua is never wasted, even when the answer is "wait."

I would look after a sixteen-year-old sister and a grieving mother. I

I believe good deeds come back to you, sometimes years later. When I 
helped a neighbour pay for his son's school fees, I forgot about it. 
Ten years later, that same boy became an engineer and found me a job 
contact when I needed it most. Noori, be kind even to people who cannot 
give you anything back. It always returns in some form.

I believe family is the only real wealth. Money comes and goes, but the 
people who sit beside you at a hospital at 3 a.m. are the ones who 
matter. Never let a small fight turn into a silence of years. I have 
seen families break over a piece of land, and I never wanted that for 
us. If you are angry with someone you love, call them first.

I believe every hard night ends in a morning. When you were scared 
before your exams, I told you the same thing our mother told me: 
"Pehle namaz parho, phir kitab kholo, baaki Allah par chor do." Do your 
part honestly and trust the rest.

Now, about support. When you were sad, I never gave lectures first. I 
listened, then I made you laugh, and only then I gave advice. That is 
how I wanted to support you, and that is how I hope you will support 
your own children. When you failed your driving test twice, I sat with 
you in the car every evening, even when I was tired from work, until 
you passed. I never told you, but I was proud of you for not giving up.

If you ever feel alone, Noori, here is what to do. First, call Imran, 
because he loves you and he is your partner now. Second, call Ayesha,

Kamran, who will pretend to be annoyed but will come anyway. And if 
nobody picks up, sit quietly, make a cup of chai, and talk to me like 
you used to on Sunday evenings. I may not answer in the old way, but I 
will be with you in everything I taught you.

Before your wedding you asked me, "Bhai jaan, what if I am not ready?" 
I told you nobody is ever completely ready. You take one step, then 
another. I told you, "Main hamesha tumhare saath hun," and I meant it. 
I still mean it now. Whenever you are afraid, remember that you were 
raised with love, you were never alone, and your strength is much 
greater than you think.

I believe in forgiveness. Holding a grudge is like carrying a heavy

years. I believe that this world is temporary, and the only things 
that last are the good you did and the people who remember you with a 
prayer.

Noori, my belief is simple: be a good person, do your duty, pray, and 
trust Allah with the rest. I believe we will meet again, and until 
then, I want you to live gently and fearlessly.
